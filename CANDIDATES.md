# One-Liner Groan-Off candidates

Scores are editorial estimates, not judge results. Each criterion is scored
1–5 (higher is better), in the judge's priority order: dad-joke fit, fast clean
landing, freshness, spoken rhythm, earned groan. Totals are a useful check;
the criteria's priority order determines the choice.

## Honest comparison

1. The pancake imagery is clear, but three familiar expressions dilute the
   final turn; pancakes going flat is less surprising than the first two puns.
2. The ladder has coherent imagery, but promotion/climbing ranks is familiar
   and three clauses make the ending predictable.
3. Spider/web/bugs is particularly well worn. The connections clause adds
   length without a stronger laugh.
4. The library book has an understandable double meaning, but two punchlines
   compete, and checking-out jokes are familiar.
5. The named daughter, dinner, and gravy boat give the wordplay a scene.
   The pun arrives at the end. Portrait wordplay is not unprecedented, so
   freshness is a 4 rather than a 5; this is newly composed wording, not a
   claim that nobody has ever used the homophone. The setup needs trimming.
6. This newly written scene lands quickly, but stirring up trouble is an
   obvious spoon joke. Its speed does not overcome its lack of freshness.
7. The courtroom scene works, but holes in a story is an obvious colander
   association. It takes longer to establish than its payoff warrants.

## Selection and one more refinement

Choose #5. It offers the strongest balance of a clean final pun and a
specific family scene. #6 is faster, but substantially more predictable.

Cut "my daughter" (the name and dinner supply the family setting), replace
"drawing a picture of our" with "sketching the," and shorten the reply.

Final: At dinner, Mia asked why I was sketching the gravy boat. 'It's a pour-trait.'

The final version improves landing and rhythm to 5 each; fit 5, freshness 4,
and groan 4 remain subjective estimates. No judge address or plea is used.
